# Penta — reference-site audit

Audit date: 17 September 2026. References were inspected as product benchmarks, not as templates to copy.

## Guardrails

- No invented reviews, user counts, press badges, partners, app-store links, social accounts or newsletter.
- No photo scan, VIN decode, live forecast, live fare or measured-wattage claim without the corresponding provider and evidence.
- Penta's strongest differentiator remains visible provenance, explicit modelling and decision boundaries.

## FixCode

References:

- [iFixit Appliances](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/Appliance): appliance hierarchy, repair guides, model identification and community depth.
- [FixLens](https://fixlens.app/): error-first entry, appliance confirmation, safe next action and explicit stop conditions.

Observed comparison:

- FixCode already makes the next safe diagnostic branch and source trace more prominent than a generic code directory.
- iFixit is stronger for disassembly, parts, tools and community repair depth. Those are not current FixCode capabilities.
- FixLens reduces entry friction with a photo-or-code promise. FixCode must not claim photo recognition until it exists.

Legitimate next improvements:

1. Put factual catalog coverage near the first input.
2. Offer example error-code starts using covered graph entities.
3. Improve type-ahead across brand, appliance and code without flattening model scope.

## AutoSpec

Reference:

- [GarageHub](https://getgaragehub.com/): ownership record, service timeline, mileage context and a vehicle-aware assistant.

Observed comparison:

- AutoSpec is stronger about fitment scope and admitting that VIN decode is unavailable.
- GarageHub is stronger once a vehicle becomes an ongoing record: logs, receipts, costs, reminders and history.
- A personality, AI mechanic, VIN import or reminders would be a new product capability, not footer polish.

Legitimate next improvements:

1. Surface real make / identity / engine coverage counts by the vehicle search.
2. Explain the two current paths clearly: inspect a known identity or start a private garage session.
3. Keep the VIN limitation secondary without disguising it as a working action.

## WearThere

References:

- [PackPoint](https://www.packpnt.com/): trip details, activities, weather-aware packing list and list customisation.
- [Vogue on fashion packing tools](https://www.vogue.com/article/fashion-tech-trip-packing): destination, dates, luggage and activity context applied to a user's wardrobe.

Observed comparison:

- WearThere has a stronger editorial destination identity and a visible capsule decision.
- PackPoint is stronger on activities and trip-list workflow.
- WearThere currently uses typical climate. It must not copy competitors' live-forecast wording.

Legitimate next improvements:

1. Add activity context where it changes the capsule fingerprint.
2. Make “typical climate” versus “current forecast” impossible to confuse at the first action.
3. Show a small, real destination example set rather than app-store or testimonial blocks that do not exist.

## ChargeMatch

References:

- [GearVerify USB-C Power Calculator](https://gearverify.com/electronics/usb-c-power-calculator/): three simple inputs, example scenarios and lowest-link explanation.
- [Cable Matters USB-C guide](https://www.cablematters.com/Blog/USB-C/usb-c-charging): plain-language protocol and cable education.

Observed comparison:

- ChargeMatch is materially deeper: named devices and chargers, protocol negotiation, cable constraints and multi-port allocation.
- GearVerify is easier to enter because its generic wattage model and examples require less terminology.
- ChargeMatch's rated-versus-measured distinction is a trust feature, not a defect to hide.

Legitimate next improvements:

1. Add graph-backed “try this pair” examples.
2. Add plain-language definitions for PD, PPS, E-marker, 3A/5A and per-port allocation.
3. Keep calculable pairs separate from SEO surfaces.

## TripCost

References:

- [Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/): mode discovery and door-to-door route comparison. Automated inspection was partially blocked; no inaccessible UI detail is treated as verified.
- [ViaMichelin Routes](https://www.viamichelin.com/routes): route alternatives, vehicle assumptions, fuel, tolls and driving preferences.

Observed comparison:

- TripCost does provide cash cost, true cost, fastest/cheapest outcomes, party-size break-even and assumptions on corridor result pages. It is not “distance only”.
- The gap is discoverability on the home surface: references expose modes and cost controls before or immediately after route entry.
- Penta is more explicit that fares and costs are modelled rather than live.

Legitimate next improvements:

1. Preview the output dimensions beside the route form: cash, true cost, fastest and break-even.
2. Explain the effect of party size before submission.
3. Add real road routing, toll or fare providers only as separately sourced capabilities; do not restyle the geodesic representation as a road map.

## Cross-product decision

The reference sites generally invest more in conversion surfaces: testimonials, apps, press, newsletters, partners and account ecosystems. Those elements should not be copied until they are real.

The next defensible UX pass is narrower:

1. factual coverage counts near primary inputs;
2. graph-backed quick examples;
3. plain-language methodology adjacent to the decision;
4. better result previews on home surfaces;
5. keep legal, cookie and evidence controls in every footer.
